# The five kinds of claim

Every rule declares what kind of statement it is making. This is the whole
vocabulary. There is nothing else to know in order to review a rule.

---

**`requirement`**

The product enforces this. Not doing it is not an available option.

*Requires at least one source.*

---

**`documented-limit`**

A boundary the product imposes, with a number. Crossing it changes what is
possible, whether or not anyone intended to cross it.

*Requires at least one source, and the limit itself stated next to the value
that was observed.*

---

**`documented-guidance`**

Microsoft recommends this. The product permits the alternative.

*Requires at least one source, and wording that does not turn the
recommendation into an obligation.*

---

**`convention`**

Widely held practice. Nobody documents it as a rule, and reasonable
organisations decline it.

*Requires a rationale. A source is optional.*

---

**`opinion`**

Our position. Stated as ours.

*Requires a rationale, and must be identifiable as the author's view.*

---

## The one thing that is not allowed

**A source does not decide the type.**

A recommendation may cite documentation. An opinion may cite documentation. A
convention may cite documentation. The source explains the claim; it does not
change what kind of claim it is.
